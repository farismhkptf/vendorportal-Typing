Please perform a full application-wide audit.

Important: this is not only a code audit.

I want you to review the app like:
- a senior product architect
- a real human user
- an operations manager
- a QA lead
- a UX specialist
- and a conservative app owner who wants clarity, correctness, and long-term system quality.

Do not implement anything yet.

Review the entire application from beginning to end, including all pages, modules, flows, forms, dashboards, roles, statuses, reports, settings, and system utilities.

For every module, ask:

1. Why does this module exist?
2. What user or business problem is it supposed to solve?
3. Who is the intended user?
4. What should the user be able to accomplish here?
5. Is the module actually serving that purpose?
6. Is the module complete enough to be useful?
7. Is anything missing, duplicated, unclear, risky, or unnecessary?
8. Are there illogical flows or states a human would notice?
9. Are there actions that appear too early, too late, or in the wrong condition?
10. Are there forms that allow incomplete, impossible, duplicated, or contradictory data?
11. Are statuses realistic and aligned with the actual process?
12. Are reports and dashboards showing trustworthy information?
13. Are permissions and visibility rules logical?
14. Does the app feel like one coherent system?

Please classify findings into:

- Critical
- High
- Medium
- Low

Also classify recommendations into:

- Must Fix
- Should Improve
- Nice to Have
- Future Version
- Do Not Build Yet

Required output:

# Application-Wide Product, Logic & QA Audit

## 1. Executive Assessment
Briefly assess whether the app feels structurally strong, scattered, incomplete, risky, or mostly sound.

## 2. Application Map
List all pages, modules, routes, and major flows reviewed.

## 3. Module Purpose Review
For each module:
- Module name
- Why it exists
- Intended user
- Core jobs it should perform
- Current condition
- Is it serving its purpose?
- What is missing
- What is unnecessary or overbuilt
- Recommended improvements
- Risk if left as-is
- Priority

## 4. Human Logic & Operational QA
List issues where the app technically works but behaves in a way a human would find confusing, illogical, incomplete, or risky.

## 5. Data Integrity & Trust Issues
List anything that can create wrong data, misleading records, failed saves, duplicate records, incorrect relationships, or false reports.

## 6. Forms, Statuses & Workflow Issues
Review whether forms, statuses, validations, transitions, and process steps make real-world sense.

## 7. Role, Permission & Visibility Issues
Review whether users can see or do the right things based on their role.

## 8. Navigation, Duplication & System Coherence
List duplicated modules, unclear navigation, overlapping tools, confusing names, and disconnected flows.

## 9. Reports, Dashboards & Summary Issues
Review whether dashboards and reports show useful, accurate, and properly explained information.

## 10. Missing Safeguards
List missing confirmations, warnings, locks, audit logs, recovery paths, duplicate checks, and risky action protections.

## 11. Enhancement Roadmap
Group recommendations into:
- Phase 1: Trust and data integrity
- Phase 2: Human logic and workflow safety
- Phase 3: Module purpose and usefulness
- Phase 4: Navigation and system coherence
- Phase 5: Reporting and dashboard clarity
- Phase 6: UX polish and future enhancements

## 12. What Not To Build Yet
List anything that may sound useful but is premature, complex, unclear, or likely to overbuild the app.

## 13. Product Clarification Needed
List questions that must be answered before implementation.

## 14. Final Recommendation
Give the safest next step.

Boundaries:
- Do not implement anything.
- Do not redesign the app.
- Do not change database schema unless clearly necessary.
- Do not assume business logic if unclear.
- Do not treat code success as product success.
- Do not overbuild.
- Challenge the plan if a smaller or safer audit approach is better.
